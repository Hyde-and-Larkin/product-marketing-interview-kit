# Playbook: the full interview loop

From "this role looks interesting" to a signed offer.

| Stage | Skill | Output | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Role comes in | `role-decoder` | Pursue / eyes open / pass, plus diligence gates | 15 min |
| Before the recruiter screen | `company-brief`, `round-prep` | Brief, tailored intro, questions to ask | 45 min |
| After every round | `debrief` | Notes, thank-you note, pipeline update | 15 min |
| Before hiring manager | `round-prep`, `mock-interview` | Matched stories, one mock run | 1 hr |
| Panel or presentation | `case-and-plan` | Deck or 30-60-90 plan | Whatever they said, not more |
| Before exec or CEO | `round-prep` | POV on the business, strategic questions | 45 min |
| Offer | `offer-review` | Questions, counter, walk-away line | 1 hr |

## Once, before your search starts

1. Fill in `context/candidate-profile.md`.
2. Build six to eight stories with `story-bank`. Make the one-screen cheat sheet.
3. Sort your numbers in `context/shareable-numbers.md`.
4. Run one `mock-interview` as a hiring manager to find your weak spots early.

## Prompt to start a new company

> Read AGENTS.md and my context. A new role came in: [link]. Run role-decoder, and if it's worth pursuing, build the company brief and prep me for the recruiter screen with [name] on [date].

## Habits that matter

- Debrief every round the same day. Patterns in where you stall are the most useful data you'll get.
- Reuse stories; don't reuse answers. Tailor the last beat to the company every time.
- Ask the strategic-vs-throughput questions early. It's the most common reason a PMM role disappoints.
- Decide your walk-away line before you get attached.
