# Story Bank

> **Part of the [Product Marketing Interview Kit](../../README.md).** Read `context/candidate-profile.md`, `context/story-bank.md`, and `context/shareable-numbers.md` first.

Most PMM interview questions are asking for one of about eight stories. Candidates who struggle usually have the experience but haven't shaped it: the result comes last, the "I" is buried in "we," and there's no number.

## The format: impact first

1. **Impact first.** Lead with the business result in one or two sentences. Interviewers decide in the first 30 seconds whether this story matters.
2. **The challenge.** Why it was hard and what was at stake for the company, not just for you.
3. **What I did.** Two or three moves, in order. "I" for your decisions, "we" for the team's work.
4. **What changed.** The result again, plus the lasting effect: a process the company adopted, a strategy that shifted, people who got promoted.

Compared to STAR, this puts the result where the listener's attention is highest. Keep STAR as the backup if an interviewer explicitly asks for it.

## The eight stories most PMM loops ask for

| Story | What they're testing |
|---|---|
| A launch that worked, end to end | GTM judgment, cross-functional execution, outcomes |
| Influencing product without authority | Whether Product will want you in the room |
| Research or data that changed a decision | Strategic POV, not just execution |
| Cross-functional conflict | Judgment and maturity under pressure |
| A hard call with incomplete information | Decision-making, ownership |
| Prioritizing when everything is urgent | Focus, saying no well |
| Tough feedback you acted on | Self-awareness, growth |
| Building or leading a team (manager roles) | Hiring, developing, structuring PMM |

One strong story can answer two or three of these. Mark which ones in the index.

## Process

1. **Interview the candidate.** For each story, ask: what was the result, what was at stake, what did you decide, who disagreed, what's the number, what's the moment you remember. Never fill in experience they didn't give you.
2. **Check numbers against `shareable-numbers.md`.** Swap anything internal-only for its relative version.
3. **Write the story** in the impact-first format, 60-90 seconds spoken (roughly 150-220 words).
4. **Drill follow-ups.** Write the five follow-ups a sharp interviewer would ask, and a short answer for each. Typical ones: how did you measure it, what would you do differently, how did you get buy-in, what role did data play, what happened next, what was your part vs. the team's.
5. **Make the memory trigger:** hook + key number + key moment. One line. That's all the candidate needs to recall the whole story under pressure.
6. **Build a cheat sheet** of all stories as triggers, one screen long.

## Weak story checklist

- [ ] The result comes after the first 30 seconds
- [ ] No number, or only activity numbers ("ran 20 campaigns")
- [ ] "We" everywhere; the candidate's own decision is unclear
- [ ] No tension: nothing was hard or at stake
- [ ] No moment: it's a summary, not a scene
- [ ] Longer than two minutes
- [ ] The same story is used for every question

## Guardrails

- The candidate's real experience only. Ask; don't invent.
- Push back on vague answers. "Improved engagement" isn't a result.
- Respect what's confidential. A relative number told confidently beats an exact one that shouldn't be shared.
