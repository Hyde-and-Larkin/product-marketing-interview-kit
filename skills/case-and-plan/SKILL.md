# Case Exercises and 30-60-90 Plans

> **Part of the [Product Marketing Interview Kit](../../README.md).** Read the `company-brief` for this company first. Pairs well with the [Product Marketing Toolkit](https://github.com/Hyde-and-Larkin/product-marketing-toolkit) skills for positioning and launch work.

## Take-homes and presentation cases

### Before starting

- **Clarify the brief.** Ask: who's the audience for the presentation, how long, what format, how much time they expect you to spend, and whether you can ask questions along the way. Asking is part of the test.
- **Cap the time.** If they say "a few hours," spend a few hours. Over-delivering on an unpaid exercise sets a bad precedent and doesn't usually change the outcome.
- **Check what you can use.** Public information only. Don't ask insiders for internal data.

### How PMM cases are usually graded

1. Did you define the customer and the problem before the product?
2. Is there a clear point of view, or a survey of options?
3. Is the reasoning visible (assumptions stated, evidence cited)?
4. Is it prioritized (what first, what not at all)?
5. Can a sales leader and a product leader both use it?
6. How do you handle pushback in the Q&A?

### Structure that works for most cases

1. **The answer** (one slide): your recommendation in a sentence
2. **The customer and the problem**: who, what pain, why now
3. **What you found**: market, competition, what customers say (sourced)
4. **The recommendation in detail**: positioning, launch plan, or GTM approach
5. **How you'd measure it**: leading and lagging indicators
6. **Risks and what you'd want to learn**: what would change your mind
7. **Assumptions**: listed, so they can disagree with them specifically

Keep it to 8-12 slides for a 30-minute slot. Leave half the time for questions.

### NDA and reuse

Don't publish or reuse a company's take-home prompt or your submission if you agreed not to. In your private copy, keep a note of what you learned, not the materials.

## 30-60-90 day plans

The most common mistake is a plan that starts executing on day one. A good plan shows you'll earn the right to change things.

### Shape

**Days 1-30: Listen and learn**
- Customer conversations (a specific number, across segments)
- Shadow sales calls and support
- One-on-ones with product, sales, CS, and marketing partners
- Audit current positioning, enablement, and launch process
- Deliverable: what you learned, and the three biggest gaps

**Days 31-60: Point of view and quick wins**
- Positioning or messaging recommendations, tested with sellers and customers
- One or two quick wins that partners asked for in month one
- A launch process or operating rhythm proposal
- Deliverable: strategy doc reviewed with your manager and key partners

**Days 61-90: Execute and measure**
- Ship the first piece of real work (a launch, a repositioning, an enablement program)
- Set up the metrics you'll be measured on
- Deliverable: 90-day review with results and next-quarter priorities

### Tailor it

- Multiple products: say how you'd split time and why
- Building a team: when you'd hire, and what roles first
- Very early stage: compress month one; they need output sooner
- Name the company's actual products, segments, and competitors from the brief. A generic plan reads as a generic candidate.

## Guardrails

- State assumptions about internal facts you can't know.
- Don't promise specific metric outcomes for a company you haven't seen inside.
